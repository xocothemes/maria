Caseworkers at b.combs support families through food assistance, tutoring, and parent education. Their old CRM treated every family as a row in a spreadsheet, and it showed in how hard the tool was to use.

## The problem

The team was spending more time keeping records current than talking to families. Three things kept coming up in field visits and diary studies:

- Notes, services, and tasks lived on separate screens with no shared context.
- Nobody could see at a glance which families had gone quiet.
- New caseworkers needed weeks of training before they felt confident.

## What I did

I shadowed six caseworkers over two weeks and mapped every task they did in a typical day. Most of the work clustered around one question: _what does this family need from me next?_

That became the organizing idea. The home screen leads with upcoming services and the families who need attention, and each family has a single profile where notes, household members, programs, and next steps sit side by side.

## Key decisions

**People before records.** Family names, faces, and the caseworker relationship lead every screen. Program codes and IDs moved into the background.

**Attention is a status, not a report.** "Follow-up due" is calculated from the last contact and shown wherever a family appears, instead of being buried in a monthly export.

**Notes stay human.** Case notes are written in plain language with light tags, so they read like a conversation history rather than a form.
