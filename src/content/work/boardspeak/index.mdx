Local decisions get made by the few people who show up to meetings. BoardSpeak set out to change that by giving neighbors a simple way to propose, discuss, and vote on what happens in their district.

## The problem

The founding team had a working prototype but a retention problem. People voted once, then never came back. The platform felt like a form to fill in, not a place to belong.

## What I did

I ran a two-week concept sprint with the founders, then designed the core loop: discover a proposal, understand it quickly, vote, and see your impact over time.

Proposals got a consistent structure with the cost, the hours, and the people affected up front. Discussions moved directly under each proposal, and a lightweight points system rewards the behaviors that make a community healthier, like thoughtful replies and showing up to town halls.

## Key decisions

**Clarity before persuasion.** Each proposal shows the same three facts first, so neighbors compare ideas on substance rather than on who wrote the best pitch.

**Reward participation, not volume.** Points come from voting, attending, and replies other people find helpful, never from posting more.

**Show the outcome.** A visible timeline from proposal to council decision closes the loop, so people see that their vote went somewhere.
